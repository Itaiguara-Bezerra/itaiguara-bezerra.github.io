The Brazilian GDP in Black and White

Markov-Switching (3 Regimes)

An advanced expansion of multi-regime time series modeling designed to capture higher structural complexity by adding an intermediate transition state, isolating volatile structural breaks from persistent economic trends.

Core Technical Framework:
  • Stochastic Granularity: Implementation of a 3-state Markovian regime architecture to separate extreme shocks from standard cyclical movements.
  • Convergence Tracking: Advanced tracking of filtered and smoothed probabilities to identify exact structural break dates across historical series.
  • Rigor Validation: Multi-language replication check to prevent mathematical convergence failures and ensure robust parameter identification.

Beyond the Average: Brazilian Inertia

Markov-Switching (2 Regimes)

A mathematical and statistical framework built to identify unobserved states and calculate conditional transition probabilities across macroeconomic regimes, mapping structural shifts without pre-defining the break-points dates.

Core Technical Framework:
  • Regime Isolation: Estimation of stochastic Markov-Switching pipelines to isolate binary states (expansion vs. contraction) inside time-varying economic indicators.
  • Matrix Calculations: Computational modeling of conditional transition probability matrices to trace structural convergence speeds.
  • Model Auditing: Strict data preparation and parameter constraints designed to avoid parameter overfitting and stabilize maximum likelihood estimations.

Energy Consumption: Forecasting using SARIMAX

SARIMAX Pipelines

A robust structural time series pipeline engineered to capture and project seasonal consumption variations inside sectorial infrastructure tracking. The framework mitigates predictive drift by embedding high-frequency external exogenous covariates directly into the forecasting engine.

Core Technical Framework:
  • Stochastic Framework: Full deployment of SARIMAX architectures optimized to capture long-run seasonal dependencies alongside short-term structural corrections.
  • Feature Engineering: Integration of dynamic exogenous variables (macroeconomic activity proxies and temperature variables) to constrain error bounds.
  • Validation Strategy: Implementation of independent statistical diagnostics (residuals autocorrelation, heteroskedasticity testing, and parameters stability audits).

Geopolitical Oil Impact

Quantitative Econometrics

An applied econometric study designed to measure the structural transmission mechanisms of global geopolitical shocks onto international commodity prices. The project focuses on isolating structural parameters and identifying pricing boundaries during volatile market corrections.

Core Technical Framework:
  • Data Ingestion & Integrity: Development of structured data ingestion pipelines tailored to process non-stationary global pricing series and volatility proxies.
  • Empirical Rigor: Implementation of formal econometric testing to ensure statistical significance and filter seasonal noise from systemic geopolitical adjustments.
  • Theoretical Alignment: Results mapped and validated to avoid black-box anomalies, ensuring output parameters strictly align with classical trade and microeconomic theories.
