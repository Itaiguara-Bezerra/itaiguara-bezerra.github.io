About Me

Economic Expertise, Technologically Expanded

Hi, I’m Itaiguara Bezerra. I am a Senior Quantitative Economist holding an MSc from the Getulio Vargas Foundation (FGV-EPGE).

Throughout my career, I have navigated complex macroeconomic cycles, modeled economic and financial indicators, and developed a deep understanding of market realities. I chose to expand my analytical boundaries not by abandoning my economic foundations, but by deliberately expanding my analytical toolkit.

By integrating statistical programming ecosystems such as Python and R into my macroeconomic framework, I have translated decades of economic experience and intuition into scalable quantitative analysis. This allows me to focus on what transforms data into meaningful economic insight: structural economic logic, mathematical discipline, and model interpretability.

Bridging Economics and Modern Data Science

My professional approach is grounded in the belief that modern computational methods should complement, rather than replace, established economic reasoning. Econometric methods and Machine Learning offer powerful tools for exploring complex economic relationships, but their usefulness ultimately depends on whether their results remain economically meaningful and interpretable.

"A machine learning model that performs exceptionally well statistically but remains economically silent can be of limited value for institutional decision-making."

Quantitative Practice & Philosophy

To bridge this methodological divide, I define my applied approach as that of a Data Economist — an economist who uses code and quantitative methodologies as natural extensions of economic theory. My objective is to ensure that advanced algorithmic frameworks, tree-based models, and dynamic econometric architectures remain guided by economic reasoning and grounded in the institutional and macroeconomic realities of the problems they are designed to address.

This platform documents applied work in macroeconomics, econometrics, and nonlinear data science. Each project is built around a simple principle: the method should serve the economic question, not the other way around.

The work presented here reflects a commitment to technical integrity, mathematical discipline, interpretability, and continuous professional evolution — qualities that become more robust with experience.
